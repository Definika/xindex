2026-11-21. The owner's call, what was read on chain for it, and what was built the same day. Nothing is deployed on
a mainnet yet, on either chain.

## 2. Why the wrapper, never the xStock

An xStock (`BackedAutoFeeTokenImplementation`) keeps each holder's shares and reports `balanceOf` as those shares
times a multiplier. A dividend, reinvested in the stock, raises the multiplier, so every holder's balance grows with
no transfer: NVDAx's multiplier read 1.0017 on 2026-11-21, after five updates. A management fee, which the contract
can charge automatically (`feePerPeriod`, zero on every xStock that day), or a reverse split lowers it, and every
balance shrinks. One externally owned key, the multiplier updater, can set any value.

An index records units per share and moves only amounts derived from them (the design rule "Value-bearing amounts are
in-kind"), so an xStock in an index would:

- strand every dividend in the index, owned by no share (CF-3): the holders lose the stock's yield;
- fall below its backing on any cut, after which `issue` refuses the index (RX-8) and the last holders to redeem come up
  short. That breaks the hub's invariant, `balanceOf(index) >= backing + claims`, without anyone moving a token.

Ondo's tokens are the opposite: the balance never changes and the dividend shows in the token's price. That is why
they fit and the xStock does not.

Backed's wrapper fits. `WrappedBackedTokenImplementation` 1.0.0 is OpenZeppelin's ERC-4626 vault over one xStock,
with a pause, a sanctions list and an EIP-712 delegated transfer. It has no mint, no burn, no fee and no rebase of its
own: its balances move only by transfer, while `convertToAssets`, the xStocks one wrapper token redeems for, carries
every multiplier change. A dividend reaches the index's holders through the wrapper's value, and a cut is a fall in
value, not a hole in the backing. The source was read from Sourcify's exact match of the implementation.

## 5. What it costs, said to the owner

- **No pool.** No Uniswap pool trades a wrapper, so the zap cannot route one. A wrapped leg mints in kind, through
  `MintRequests`, or from an executor's stock, as Tether Gold and Ondo's stocks did. The answer the owner was given on
  2026-11-21 to "how do big protocols get tokens with no pools into an index": a filler with mint access at the issuer,
  an authorized participant, which is the team's collaboration with Backed to settle, and counsel's.
- **The keeper values a wrapper at its feed times its rate** (built the same day, section 8). Until then every
  wrapped id sat on `SHARE_PRICED_IDS` (RX-2) and the bot left those legs alone. What is left of that cost: the rate
  is Backed's to set, and the bot's only guard is a twenty-minute wait on a move of more than 1%.
- **The parties.** Backed's custodian holds the shares. The 2-of-3 Safe can replace the code of every wrapper and
  every xStock, which is every power at once. The multiplier key, one externally owned key, can set any multiplier
  above zero, so it can reprice every wrapped leg to almost nothing; nothing on our side bounds it. Where that key owns a wrapper (53 on
  Ethereum) it can also name a sanctions list that refuses the index, which freezes the leg: holders still leave with
  `redeemSkipping`, and the leg's claims wait. The app labels every wrapped leg Custodial and Pausable, with Backed
  as its issuer.
- **Hours.** 91 feeds publish only while the US market is open, so the keeper values those legs only then (RX-1).
- **Eligibility.** xStocks are not offered to US persons, and minting them is for Backed's onboarded participants.

## 8. The keeper's price of a wrapper

Built on 2026-11-21, in the keeper's bot alone; no contract changed.

**The rule.** A wrapper's feed prices one xStock. The bot reads `convertToAssets(one whole wrapper)` from the
wrapper, at the block every other read of its round is pinned to, and values the wrapper at the feed's price times
that rate, the price rounded down and its confidence interval up (`bot/rates.mjs`, `M.atRate`). It values a wrapper
at nothing else: the units it names for a fill, the stock it delivers and keeps, a request's worth, an index's.
`MAINNET_WRAPPED` (`bot/price-ids.mjs`) names the 109, pinned against `script/Listings.sol`,
`script/MainnetAddresses.sol` and `script/PythIds.sol`, and `WRAPPED_TOKENS` adds a wrapper the catalogue does not
hold.

**It fails closed by feed.** `SHARE_PRICED_IDS` keeps every wrapper's feed beside the twelve Ondo ones: a token at
one of those feeds is valued only when the bot reads its rate, and is otherwise a share-priced leg, left alone
(RX-2, `sharePricedLegs`). So a wrapper added through `PRICE_IDS` alone, a redeployed one or a 110th, is not valued
at its feed as if it were one xStock; an independent review of the first draft, which exempted wrappers by address
alone, found that. A new wrapper at a feed of its own, on neither list, would still be: `PRICE_IDS` says so.

**What was read**, from the 109 wrappers at block 26,464,080 through that module:

| Wrapper | Rate | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 95 of the 109 | 1 | Empty: nothing wrapped yet |
| wAAPLx, wMSFTx, wQQQx, wMCDx, wSPYx, wJNJx | 1.0024 to 1.0149 | Dividends since the first deposit |
| wSTRCx | 1.0824 | The same, a preferred's dividends |
| wNVDAx | 0.9918 | 3.130 wrappers over 3.104 NVDAx: less than was deposited, which only the multiplier does |
| wGOOGLx | 78.9 | 0.0144 wrappers over 1.136 GOOGLx; how it came to hold them was not read |
| wHDx | 2 | Empty, with 1 raw unit of HDx left in it |
| wMETAx | 1,001 | Empty, with 1,000 raw units of METAx left in it |

So a wrapper is not one share, and the rate is not the xStock's multiplier (AAPLx's read 1.0038 against the
wrapper's 1.0024). Three things follow:

- **Anyone who prices a wrapper reads its rate.** The keeper does now. The app must, and the team naming a new
  index's units must: an index of wGOOGLx created at one share's price per unit would hold 79 times its weight.
- **An empty wrapper's rate is anyone's to raise.** OpenZeppelin's vault counts one virtual share, so a few raw
  units left in an empty wrapper multiply its rate, for nothing: wMETAx's 1,001 cost 1,000 raw units. It is the rate
  the next deposit mints at, so it is true, and it can only go up that way. A wrapper that holds deposits moves
  only by a real gift to its holders, or by the multiplier.
- **Down is the issuer's alone.** Only the multiplier lowers a rate, and one key of Backed's sets it.

**The guard.** The bot reads the rate again 100 blocks earlier (`RATE_LOOKBACK_BLOCKS`, about twenty minutes, and
inside the recent state a full node keeps; a node that cannot serve it fails the read, which values nothing), two
calls to the node for all of an index's wrappers. If the two differ by more than 1% (`MAX_RATE_MOVE_BPS`), or
either read fails, or the rate is zero, the wrapper has no price: its leg keeps its units, no request on an index
holding it is filled, none of it counts as stock, and the log says so once. A dividend moves a rate by a percent
or less; a split moves it by the split's factor at a moment the share's feed need not share; a mistaken or stolen
multiplier key moves it anywhere. What the guard is: twenty minutes for an operator to look and to set that coin's
stock allowance to zero. What it is not: a judgment on the new rate, which the bot takes once the move is that
old, or a bound on a rate that drifts under 1% per twenty minutes. The bot's loss to a wrong rate is bounded by
the stock and request allowances the executor's owner sets, as its loss to a wrong price is.

**What a wrong rate costs, and whom.** The index reads no price and no rate, so nothing here reaches a holder's
backing. A rate set too high makes the bot overpay for wrappers and for an index's shares; too low, it gives them
away. Both come out of the keeper's float and stock.

**Hours.** Unchanged: 91 wrappers are priced at a US-hours feed, a price older than a minute values nothing
(RX-1), so the bot moves those legs only while the US market is open.

**Tests.** `keeper/test/unit/rates.test.mjs` (the reads, the look-back, the bound each way, a failed read, one read
per round, a chain younger than the look-back, which legs are share-priced), `math.test.mjs` (`rateWad`,
`rateSteady`, `atRate` and their rounding), `config.test.mjs` and `price-ids.test.mjs` (the lists against the
Solidity, `WRAPPED_TOKENS`), and three cases in `keeper/test/harness/requests.test.mjs`. Mutants X1 to X16, all
killed. The bot's unit suite has 247 tests. Where `keeper.mjs` and the request filler apply the rate has no unit
suite, as none of the bot's wiring has: the harness covers the filler's, and the end-to-end test the keeper's.

**End to end.** The keeper's end-to-end test (`keeper/test/e2e/run.mjs`) holds WRP, an index of WETH and wGOOGLx.
The wrapper is got the way anyone gets one: on the fork GOOGLx's minter mints the xStock and the index's creator
deposits it, since anvil's `deal` does not find the wrapper's balances and a dealt balance would redeem for
nothing. With GOOGLx's feed up a fifth the index sells the wrapper, which no pool trades, and the keeper started
the auction, filled it at a bar of 0.0038, kept the wrapper as stock and booked it at $3,876.74: its feed times
its rate of 78.9. At the feed alone it would have booked a seventy-ninth of that, and the fill, which pays WETH
for the wrapper, would never have paid. 42 of 42 on a mainnet fork at block 26,441,062, with the rate's look-back read served by the fork.
