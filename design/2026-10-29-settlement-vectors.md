# The settlement vectors: an exact model, replayed (2026-10-29)

Step 3 of the logic-hardening plan agreed on 2026-10-29, built the same day as step 1
(`design/2026-10-29-rebalance-state-machine.md`): a model of `IWeightedMath.settlement` in exact rationals, written
from the spec rather than from the contract, a seeded generator of vectors across every path the arithmetic has, and
a test that replays each vector through `WeightedMath` and demands the model's answer word for word. The contract is
unchanged: the record describes `src/WeightedMath.sol` at `ccf1da4`, and a change to the arithmetic changes the model,
the vectors and the test in the same commit.

The three files:

- `test/vectors/settlement.py`: the model and the generator, Python 3 with the standard library alone
  (`fractions.Fraction` for the arithmetic, `random.Random(SEED)` for the inputs, `json` for the output).
- `test/vectors/settlement.json`: 365 vectors, each an input and the model's outcome, regenerated byte for byte by
  `python3 -I test/vectors/settlement.py > test/vectors/settlement.json` from the repository root.
- `test/WeightedMath.vectors.t.sol`: the replay, two tests.

## 1. Why a model in exact rationals, and why committed vectors

`WeightedMath.settlement` is a chain of `Math.mulDiv` calls, each with a rounding direction, over numbers up to
2^96 units times 1e45 of price. A unit test checks a handful of hand-computed cases; a fuzz test checks properties
(`testFuzz_R04_theExecutorDeliversMuTimesWhatItTakes`, the invariant suite's `muBoundBroken` and
`turnoverBroken`). Neither says that every intermediate rounds where the spec says and nowhere else. A model that
computes each quantity once, as an exact fraction, and rounds it once, in the direction the spec names, does: any
rounding the contract applies twice, any intermediate it truncates, any cap it applies in the wrong place, and any
overflow it hides, shows as a mismatch on some vector. The model never calls `mulDiv` and never truncates a product.

The vectors are committed rather than produced by `vm.ffi` at test time: CI then needs no Python, a reviewer reads
the inputs and the expected answers in the pull request, and no test can run a program (`ffi` stays off, A7-18's
spirit). The test reads the file through `vm.readFile`, so `foundry.toml`'s `fs_permissions` gains `./test/vectors`,
read-only, and `FOUNDRY_TOML_HASH` in `test/Repo.t.sol` moved with it (A7-12). The whole file is kept in memory
during a test: held in storage, every parse read its 217 KB back word by word and the test ran out of gas.

## 2. The model

The inputs are the contract's: `mu`, one price per component (USD per raw unit, scaled by 1e36), the share supply,
the scale every recorded unit carries, the recorded units, the weights in basis points and the keep mask. With `M`
the moving components, `V = Σ_M u_i · p_i` and `W = Σ_M w_i`:

1. **Targets** (the spec's step 4). A moving leg's target is `t_i = ⌈w_i · V / (W · p_i)⌉`; a kept leg's is its
   current units.
2. **The share** (step 5). `s = 1e18`, lowered to `⌊u_i · MAX_TURNOVER / (u_i − t_i)⌋` for every shrinking leg and to
   `⌊u_i · MAX_GROWTH / (t_i − u_i)⌋` for every growing one, each bound rounded down at 1e18, so no shrinking leg
   gives up more than 20% of its own units and no growing leg takes in more than 100% of its own before `μ`
   (V5-M2, RX-15). The release is `R = Σ_shrinking (u_i − t_i) · p_i`.
3. **The minimum trade** (V5-L2, V5R-1). `⌊R · s / 1e18⌋ < ⌈V · MIN_TRADE / 1e18⌉` is `NothingToRebalance`. The spec
   names the rule, not its roundings; the model rounds the release down and the minimum up, both against the fill,
   which is the direction of "a fill must trade", and the contract agrees on every vector, the thirty boundary
   ones included (section 3).
4. **The steps** (step 6). `g = ⌈μ · s / 1e18⌉`; a shrinking leg moves to `u_i − ⌊(u_i − t_i) · s / 1e18⌋`, a growing
   leg to `u_i + ⌈(t_i − u_i) · g / 1e18⌉`, and a growing leg's new units above `2^96 − 1` are a revert naming them,
   the first such leg in component order (RX-61).
5. **The amounts** (step 7). Each is the leg's change in backing, `supply · Δu · scale / 1e36`, rounded down when it
   leaves and up when it comes back (SA-07).
6. **The redo.** When `μ` is not `MU_MAX` and no leg sends anything out, steps 4 and 5 are redone at `MU_MAX`: nobody
   is paid for an empty or dust index (R14, XA-27).

A vector's outcome is one of: settles, with `μ` used, the new units and both amount arrays; `NothingToRebalance`;
or the overflow, with the value the revert names.

## 3. The generator

Every kind starts from an index on its weights at prices and a value per share within what the list holds (USD per
raw unit scaled by 1e36 between 1e15, a $1,000 coin with 18 decimals, and 1e30, a $1 coin with 6; $100 to $10,000
per whole token), with 2 to 12 components, random weights summing to 10,000, a supply between 1e15 and 1e30, the
scale at one and `μ` anywhere in [`MU_MIN`, `MU_MAX`]; then it moves one thing:

| Kind | What it changes | Vectors | Settle | Refuse | Overflow |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| random | any units in [1e4, 2^96 >> 10] and any price up to 1e45: far from the weights | 20 | 0 | 20 | 0 |
| near | half the legs' units moved by up to 1% either way | 70 | 40 | 30 | 0 |
| far | every leg's units scaled by 0.3 to 3 | 60 | 59 | 1 | 0 |
| collapsed | one leg's price divided by 2 to 50: the growth cap binds (RX-15) | 30 | 25 | 5 | 0 |
| flat | on the weights exactly | 20 | 0 | 20 | 0 |
| kept | a keep mask leaving two moving, every leg scaled by 0.5 to 2 | 40 | 40 | 0 | 0 |
| dust | a supply of 0, 1 or 2 wei-shares: every amount out rounds to zero, the fill is redone at `MU_MAX` | 20 | 20 | 0 | 0 |
| scaled | a scale between 1e10 and 1e18 | 30 | 30 | 0 | 0 |
| huge | one growing leg between 0.9 · 2^96 and 2^96 − 1, priced so its target is well above it | 15 | 0 | 0 | 15 |
| edges | `μ` at `MU_MIN`, one or `MU_MAX`; a supply of 1, 1e18 or 1e30 | 30 | 30 | 0 | 0 |
| boundary | pairs a raw unit apart on either side of the minimum trade, found by bisection on one leg's units | 30 | 15 | 15 | 0 |
| quantum | three legs priced at one quantum of value each, searched so the minimum-trade check's two roundings decide (section 5, V15 and V16) | 10 | 0 | 10 | 0 |

375 vectors: 259 settle, 101 refuse, 15 overflow; 16 are redone at `MU_MAX`, 33 keep a leg, 30 carry a scale below
one, 6 have no supply, and in 86 the turnover cap binds (a leg gives up 19.99% or more of its units). The generator
asserts minimum counts for each, and so does the replay, from the vectors themselves.

The twenty `random` vectors and the one `far` refusal are the share-zero stall: a growing leg whose target is more
than 1e18 times its units lowers `s` to zero, so the fill trades nothing and reverts `NothingToRebalance`, whatever
the other legs would release. That is V5-L3, the known limitation, in numbers: a leg that collapses by a factor of
1e18 against the rest stops the fills until the guardian keeps it. A collapse by 1e6 does the same; by 50 it does
not, which is why the `collapsed` kind divides by at most 50.

## 4. The replay

`test_everyVectorSettlesAsTheExactModelSays` calls `WeightedMath.settlement` on each vector inside `try`, and
requires the model's `μ`, units and amounts on a settlement, or the model's exact revert data on a refusal or an
overflow, naming the vector and its kind on a mismatch. `test_theVectorsReachEveryPath` recounts the outcomes and
the paths from the vectors, not from the file's summary, so a regenerated file that lost a path fails, and checks
three facts the model owes the spec on every settled vector: `μ` used is the auction's or `MU_MAX`, no leg both
sends and receives, and a kept leg's units do not move. The two take 49M and 35M gas, under a second together.

The first run of the replay passed on every vector without a change to either side.

## 5. The mutants

Each mutant is one edit to `src/WeightedMath.sol`, applied to a byte copy, run against the replay alone
(`forge test --match-path test/WeightedMath.vectors.t.sol`) and restored. They are the mutants AGENTS.md lists under
the settlement's rounding, plus the caps, the scale and a constant.

| Mutant | Edit in `src/WeightedMath.sol` | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | targets round down | killed |
| V2 | a shrinking leg's step rounds up | killed |
| V3 | a growing leg's step rounds down | killed |
| V4 | the growth factor `μ · s` rounds down | killed |
| V5 | no turnover cap | killed |
| V6 | no growth cap | killed |
| V7 | no minimum trade | killed |
| V8 | the minimum trade checked on the release before the cap (V5R-1 undone) | killed |
| V9 | `amountsOut` round up | killed |
| V10 | `amountsIn` round down | killed |
| V11 | no redo at `MU_MAX` | killed |
| V12 | no overflow check on a growing leg's units | killed |
| V13 | the turnover bound on the share rounds up | killed |
| V14 | the growth bound on the share rounds up | killed |
| V15 | the minimum trade's minimum rounds down | killed, by the `quantum-minimum` vectors alone |
| V16 | the minimum trade's release rounds up | killed, by the `quantum-release` vectors alone |
| V17 | a kept leg's value counted in `V` | killed |
| V18 | the scale ignored in the amounts | killed |
| V19 | `MAX_TURNOVER` at 25% | killed |

All nineteen die, every one by `test_everyVectorSettlesAsTheExactModelSays`. Since 2026-10-31 they are committed as
exact edits in `test/mutants/mutants.json` with a twentieth, V20 (a shrinking leg paid as a difference of floored
backings, from AGENTS.md's list), and CI's mutation gate runs them (`test/mutants/run.py`). V15 and V16 survived the first 365
vectors: the two roundings of the minimum-trade check change a decision only when the capped release and the minimum
lie within one quantum of value of each other (a raw unit of a component times a unit of its scaled price, 1e-36
USD at the scale of the listed prices), and no vector built on listed prices lands there. The ten `quantum` vectors
price three legs at one quantum each and are searched for the two windows (section 3); with them both die.

## 6. Findings

- **The minimum-trade check's roundings are immaterial at any listed price.** The release rounds down and the
  minimum up before they are compared (section 2, step 3). The two roundings move the decision only within one
  quantum of value, 1e-36 USD at the listed prices, which no fill of a real index lands on: V15 and V16 survived
  every vector built on listed prices and die only on legs priced at one quantum. The contract's directions are
  the holders' and are now written down; nothing to change.
- **No vector disagrees.** The contract's chained `mulDiv`s give the exact roundings on all 365, the thirty
  boundary vectors included, so the three roundings the spec leaves unnamed (the minimum trade's two and the growth
  factor's) are the ones the contract applies, and are now written down in section 2.
- **The share-zero stall has a number.** A growing leg whose target exceeds 1e18 times its units stops every fill
  (section 3). The hub's answer is the guardian's keep mask (V5-L3); nothing to change, and the figure belongs in
  the known limitations.
- **Nothing to change in the contract.** The four release hashes stand.
