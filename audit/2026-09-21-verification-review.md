# Verification review of 2026-09-21

A review of the whole repository for logic and architectural errors, at `main` `9116cb8`, after the 2026-09-16 whole-repo
audit (`audit/triage.md`) and the 2026-09-18 logic review (`audit/2026-09-18-logic-review.md`). It read the four core
contracts end to end, mutation-tested the offline suite (34 hand-picked mutants of `src/`), and then put every finding
through twelve adversarial refuters (three lenses each: code correctness, prior art, exploitability) and eight
executable proof-of-concept suites, each in its own copy of the repository. Findings are `VR-01` to `VR-06`; their
regression tests carry the id.

Each fix was checked the way `audit/triage.md` asks: the test fails on the old code (or on the mutant it is meant to
catch) and passes on the new. Two of the review's own first-pass claims were wrong and were corrected by the
verification stage; both corrections are recorded in the rows below rather than quietly dropped.

The fork suites could not be run: the environment has no mainnet RPC. `ISwapRouter02`'s struct layouts are therefore
unverified, and no local mock can substitute for them, because `test/mocks/` is written against the same struct
definition `IndexZap` uses. Run `MAINNET_RPC_URL=... forge test --match-path 'test/fork/*'` before deploying.

| VR-01 | INFO | The capped methodology's top weight is a step function of the caps | DOCUMENTED | `_cappedWeights` assigns rank 1 a flat `CAPPED_TOP_WEIGHT` (`src/IndexRule.sol:413`) rather than capping it there, and `_top` (`:437`) breaks a tie by position, so crossing the top-two tie moves 12.5 percentage points between two of five otherwise equal components. Measured end to end: an index sitting exactly on its methodology (units 150e18 / 87.5e18 x4) reports turnover 0 and `startRebalance(0)` reverts `TurnoverTooLow(0)`; after one tick (0.01%) on one pool, turnover is 124994750091873392 and `startRebalance(0)` succeeds from any address. `MIN_TURNOVER` is 0.5% and cannot filter it; the half-to-double clip does not bind (149.99e18 against a 175e18 ceiling); and the snapshot price checks do not fire, because the shift is persistent and one tick is far inside `MAX_TICK_DEVIATION`. Not fixed in code: the 30% assignment is a recorded product decision (`src/libraries/Methodology.sol:11-13`, ARCHITECTURE section 2, AGENTS.md) and is already asserted at `test/IndexRule.t.sol:386-390`; changing it to a cap, or adding a rank-1 hysteresis band, alters the recorded methodology and is the owner's call. What was unrecorded is the size of the step and that no gate filters it: README known limitations and ARCHITECTURE section 5 now say so. Tests: `test_VR01_oneWeiOfCapMovesTheTopWeight`, `test_VR01_aOneTickRankFlipClearsMinTurnover`. The crossing is honest and the trade is value-neutral at snapshot prices, so the cost is churn plus the already-documented lone-filler spread. |
| VR-02 | INFO | A kept component's unchecked price reshapes the components that do trade | DOCUMENTED | `_snapshotMarket` prices a kept component with `_price` (`src/IndexRule.sol:366`), which applies neither the `minLiquidity` floor nor `MAX_TICK_DEVIATION` that `_snapshotPrice` applies to every component that moves. The docs said the capped methodology "only ranks it". That understates it: the kept cap also picks rank 1 through `_top` and sits in the `pool` denominator (`:408`) that every non-top weight divides by, while rank 1's weight is a constant, so it reshapes the free components' targets. Measured: caps 50/20/15/10/5, units 100e18 each, `keepMask = 1 << 4`; moving only the kept pool's tick to 40,000 takes weights from (0.30, 0.20, 0.20, 0.20, 0.10) to (0.20, 0.20, 0.18, 0.12, 0.30) and targets from 133.33/88.89/88.89/88.89 to 114.29/114.29/102.86/68.57, about a tenth of the basket re-targeted. The contract's own `turnover` *falls* over the same move (8.3333% to 7.8571%), so `MIN_TURNOVER` cannot see the distortion -- the review's first pass had this backwards and said turnover rose. Reachable only by the owner or guardian (`keepMask != 0`, `src/IndexToken.sol:234-237`), bounded by the half-to-double clip, and cancellable during the notice period. Not fixed in code: excluding kept components from the capped weights, as the uncapped methodology already does (`:364`), would leave fewer than `MIN_CAPPED_COMPONENTS` ranks to place the remainder in, which is the feasibility condition the capped rule needs -- the accepted residue of XA-05 stands, now documented as more than ranking in the `IndexRule` header, `IIndexRule.targetUnits`, ARCHITECTURE sections 2 and 5. Tests: `test_VR02_aKeptComponentsPriceReshapesTheActiveTargets`, `test_VR02_theUncappedMethodologyIsImmune`. |
| VR-03 | INFO | The rule's targets bought back less value than the shrinking legs released | FIXED | `_clip` turned a component's share of value into units with a flooring `mulDiv`, losing up to one raw unit per component. The component that value came from was already pinned at its floor, so LR-02's rule that a floor-pinned component is never handed value back meant nothing reallocated the residue: the index sent it out as `amountsOut` with no matching `amountsIn`, and `_checkBacking` verifies backing, not value. Two consequences, both measured. An executor whose `onXindexRebalance` body is empty settled a rebalance and kept 1.979882832792812656 raw units per growing component -- 1.98x the one-raw-unit bound the rounding of `amountsIn` alone implies, because the two truncations stack; the review's first pass asserted the one-unit bound and was wrong. And with an honest executor at multiplier exactly 1.0 (no auction discount at all) a coarse-unit basket paid out 39,555,418,500,000,000,000,000 wei of value against 19,780,857,560,236,165,042,936 received, a 49.99% gift. `_clip` now rounds the share back into units upwards, clamped to the ceiling the arithmetic already guarantees, so the residue sits on the executor's side of the trade where every other rounding in the system already sits. After the fix the same basket receives 39,561,715,120,472,330,085,875 against 39,555,418,500,000,000,000,000 paid out, and the free take is 0 across the whole swept tick range. Absolute exposure before the fix was governed by how coarse an index's per-share units are: a realistic control (18-decimal, WBTC-like 8-decimal, 6-decimal) measured the whole take at 1,250,352,879 wei, a fraction of a cent, which is why this is INFO; `createIndex` only checks `units[i] != 0`, so coarse-unit indexes are reachable. README's claim that such a settlement "moves no holder's value" was measurably false (1.276% of that basket) and is corrected. Tests: `test_VR03_anHonestFillAtParityNeverPaysTheExecutor`, `test_VR03_anEmptyExecutorCannotSettleADustRebalance`; both fail on the old rule with the numbers above. |
| VR-04 | INFO | Cancelling an expired auction re-armed the restart cooldown from scratch | FIXED | `cancelRebalance` checked only `_pending.eta == 0`, so it accepted a proposal whose auction had already expired and set `restartAllowedAt = block.timestamp + REBALANCE_DELAY`. An expiry already holds strangers off until its own deadline plus `REBALANCE_DELAY`, so the cancel pushed that out again -- by one second just after the expiry, and by a full `REBALANCE_DELAY` when timed at the moment strangers were about to be let in (172,800 seconds, measured). The cancel is still allowed, because it clears the stale proposal the views report, but it now takes the later of the hold-off already in force and the one the expiry granted, and arms a fresh `REBALANCE_DELAY` only while the auction is live. This grants the owner and guardian no power they lacked -- they can cancel any live proposal, and chaining start and cancel holds strangers off at one transaction per two days either way -- and moves no holder value, hence INFO. Tests: `test_VR04_cancellingAnExpiredAuctionDoesNotExtendTheHoldOff` (fails on the old code with 3196801 against 3024001), `test_VR04_anExpiredCancelReportsTheExpirysOwnHoldOff`, `test_VR04_cancellingALiveAuctionStillArmsTheFullCooldown`. |
| VR-05 | INFO | The executor callback observes an intermediate unbacked state | DOCUMENTED | `executeRebalance` writes the new units and bumps `rebalanceNonce` before it moves any token, so for the length of the payout loop and the callback an outside reader sees units the index does not yet back. This is deliberate checks-effects-interactions and no value is at risk: every function that mints, burns, pays a claim or touches a rebalance shares the index's guard for the whole call, which a probe covering all twelve nonpayable ABI functions confirmed. `IRebalanceExecutor.onXindexRebalance` now says so, next to the existing instruction never to price an index off `balanceOf(index)`. No test of its own: fixed by documentation, and `test_R11_theExecutorCallbackCannotReenter` already covers the guard. |
| VR-06 | INFO (tests) | Two load-bearing expressions in the rule survived the whole suite | FIXED | Mutation-testing 34 hand-picked mutants of `src/` against the committed suite left six alive. Four are not test gaps: `IndexToken.sol:332`'s `+ totalClaims[token]` is a provably equivalent mutant (given the backing invariant, `held - amountsOut + amountsIn` always dominates `backing + claims`, so the term can never bind in normal operation -- it is a real safety net against a component that can seize balances, and is kept); `_share`'s `below >= above` is an unreachable tie (0 occurrences in 400,000 randomised baskets, and identical targets when forced); `_cappedWeights`' `weight < CAPPED_MAX_WEIGHT` differs only at exact equality; and `_fill`'s `Math.saturatingSub` was never reached in 110,000 runs across three searches plus a `pinnedValue > value` probe, so it is better described as unreachable defensive code than as untested. The other two are genuine and now have kill-tests. `_clip`'s `Math.max(..., basket.lo[i])` (`src/IndexRule.sol:567`) is what stops a component on the WAD value scale, whose weight and whole holding both round away, from being targeted at zero units -- backing the index would no longer owe on a component it still holds, and which no later rebalance could restore, since every target is bounded by half to double of zero. `_bound`'s `Math.max(..., 1)` (`:482`) is what keeps such a basket's `activeValue` non-zero instead of reverting `DegenerateBasket`. Tests: `test_VR06_theClipFloorKeepsAZeroShareAtItsFloor`, `test_VR06_theBoundValueFloorKeepsADustBasketAlive`; each fails under exactly its own mutant and passes under the other. The `Math.min(basket.hi[i], ...)` added with VR-03 never bound in 20,000 fuzz runs and is the bound the arithmetic already gives (`share <= ceilingValue` implies `ceil(share * scale / p) <= hi`); it is kept as insurance against a later change to `hi`. |

Reviewed and found sound, each with an executed proof-of-concept rather than by argument: flash-mint neutrality around
a fill, at multipliers 1.5, mid-auction and 0.9, with tiny and very large flash amounts, a separate executor, open
claims and an empty index (15 tests, including a 512-run fuzz); the backing invariant and the exact sum of `claimOf`
against `totalClaims`, under an independent stateful harness at 64 runs by depth 300 plus two scripted torture tests;
the `held` bound in `_checkBacking` against donations; the water-filling's bounds, value conservation and absence of
panics over 60,000 fuzz runs across the full tick range with units to `uint96` max, every keep mask and both
methodologies, plus 3,000 swept baskets; the zap's hop-by-hop accounting, cross-leg paths through another component,
budget algebra, leftover allowances and permit front-running (11 tests); and the reentrancy guard's coverage of all
twelve nonpayable entry points, called both from the executor callback and from a component transfer hook.

Still unverified, with the test that would settle each: `ISwapRouter02`'s struct layouts (the fork suite, before any
deploy -- the highest residual risk on the list, because a layout slip misroutes funds and every local gate passes);
the factory's listing and guardian timelocks and the swap-and-pop whitelist array under a stateful invariant across
`LISTING_DELAY`, `PROPOSAL_WINDOW` and `GUARDIAN_DELAY`; `ConfigureWhitelist._action` across every listing state; and
the `weight == CAPPED_MAX_WEIGHT` boundary of VR-06's mutant (e), which needs a hand-built cap vector, not fuzzing.
