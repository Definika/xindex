# Red team of 2026-10-18

An orchestrated red team of the Xindex contracts, scripts and keeper on `pre-mainnet` at `77a0f12` ("Before mainnet:
read OP-PIP-132, Ondo's corporate actions and the 2026-10-10 cohort's source; list 93"), run on 2026-10-18 and
2026-10-19. Its scope was `src/`, `script/`, the keeper (`keeper/bot/`, `keeper/keeper.mjs`,
`keeper/src/KeeperExecutor.sol`), the tests, the records the code is held against (`AGENTS.md`, `README.md`,
`ARCHITECTURE.md`, `keeper/README.md`, the design and audit records), and the outside facts the design leans on: Pyth
on Ethereum after OP-PIP-132, Ondo's and Tether Gold's token rules, the listed and queued Wormhole copies and their
home-chain sources, Ethereum's coming upgrades, and Foundry v1.8.1.

## Method

- **Hunters by lens, three rounds.** Twenty-nine lenses in the first round. Fourteen follow-ups in two more rounds,
  eight and six, chosen by the lead from the leads and gaps the first round left open: Pyth's real post-OP-PIP-132
  updates (is the slot still one price?); shared ledgers and standing allowances (SNX aliases, Tether's deprecation,
  AXS's gateway, ERC-2771); Ondo GM and Tether Gold, their restrictions and corporate actions against the hub's legs;
  bridged and custodial legs, still redeemable and still backed; Pyth Core's lifetime, feed retirement and publisher
  concentration; a stateful exact-integer model of the whole hub in coarse and extreme regimes; Foundry v1.8.1's
  script semantics against `DeployChecks`; one thin-coin push against many indexes; Pyth on Ethereum, settled on
  chain; the queued Wormhole copies, screened at source; a stateful model of `BasketBuilder`'s escrows; Ethereum
  upgrades and the toolchain against immutability; stranger levers in listed tokens' code; and a kill-test audit of
  the load-bearing checks. They produced 164 candidates.
- **Dedup** folded them into 99 findings, `F-01` to `F-99`.
- **Verifiers by claimed severity**, each reading the code afresh at `77a0f12`. A finding claimed medium or above got
  up to four: two code-truth verifiers, one tracing the mechanism and one working the numbers; an exploitability
  verifier, which asks who gains, who loses and under what precondition on mainnet; and a prior-knowledge verifier,
  which searches the audit records, triages, retirement records, known limitations, design records, NatSpec and tests
  for the same mechanism. A finding claimed low got three (mechanism, exploitability, prior knowledge) and an
  informational one two (mechanism, prior knowledge). 268 verdicts came back.
- **Survival.** A finding survives when at least one code-truth verifier confirms or partly confirms it, the
  exploitability verifier does not judge it unexploitable, and the prior-knowledge verifier finds no record that still
  covers it. A record whose bound or claim the finding breaks (the verifier's "recorded but broken") does not cover
  it. Both code-truth verifiers refuting makes a finding refuted, a covering record makes it already recorded, and a
  confirmed finding judged unexploitable is contested. Each finding carries the severity the verifiers settled on,
  not the hunter's claim: F-01 and F-02 were claimed high and F-46 low, and all three are medium here.
- **Proofs of concept**, one at a time, for the four medium survivors only, on new untracked files: node scripts in
  the session's scratchpad, which run the keeper bot's own modules unmodified with only the chain mocked, and Foundry
  tests under `test/poc/` and `keeper/test/poc/` against the repository's own contracts and mocks. Each was run with
  the command its section quotes and nothing else. No RPC, no fork suite, no Hermes key.
- **Cut short to save tokens.** The run was stopped early to save tokens. Seven informational findings (F-90, F-93 and
  F-95 to F-99) lack one or both of their checks and are listed as unverified; two informational survivors (F-92 and
  F-94) had their mechanism checked but no prior-knowledge check; and no low finding got a proof of concept, so every
  low survivor rests on reading alone.
- **Not done.** The hunting and verifying rounds were read-only: no forge run, no fork RPC, no Hermes key. Some lenses
  read public pages, explorers and verified sources and saved them in the scratchpad (`redteam/web/`,
  `redteam/bridged/`), and F-24's verifier read EIP-8037, EIP-7773 and EIP-8261 online. Beyond the proofs' own
  commands nothing was run: no fork suite, no keeper end-to-end test, no gate. No external auditor took part.

Claims are reported as the verifiers left them: a finding a proof of concept confirmed is stated as confirmed, the
proof's output is quoted where it is decisive, and a finding nobody ran is marked as traced by reading.

**Result.** No path found lets an unprivileged party take funds from index holders, from `BasketBuilder` escrows or
from `MintRequests` escrows outside the documented bounds. Where holders can lose, the loss stays inside the per-fill
unit cap or the μ band, or it follows from an act of an issuer, of a bridge or the chain behind a wrapped copy, of
Pyth, of the owner or of the guardian. The lenses on core access control, reentrancy, governance and timelocks,
arithmetic and parameter validation found no fund-loss candidate. Four documented statements about those parties fall
short: five named Wormhole guardians, not 13 of 19, can mint most of the wrapped copies (RX-6); an issuer that can
mint its coin reaches the whole index through the fixed weights, up to a fifth of every other leg per fill (RX-15); an
Ondo token's termination is a loss, not "a stalled rebalance" (RX-29); and the chain a copy is wrapped from is a party
of its own, whose shutdown of Sei's Cosmos side would leave the SEI copy unredeemable (RX-32).

Sixty-seven findings survive: four medium, thirty-two low and thirty-one informational. Three of the four mediums are
in Xindex's own keeper, the first filler by the owner's call of 2026-10-18, and put only its operator's money at risk,
the USDC float and the stock it serves poolless coins from. Its request path trades at Hermes' last price, however old
(RX-1); it values the twelve share-priced Ondo legs at the share's feed, a tenth of their worth after a 10-for-1 split
(RX-2); and a settlement that lands before one of its request fills re-sizes the stock that fill moves, which the USDC
floor cannot see (RX-4). The fourth is the hub's: μ keeps falling while a US-hours stock feed is shut, so an index
holding one of the six Equity.US legs is first fillable at the next open near `MU_MIN`, and a stranger can time a start
so that the auction's last block is the only fillable one (RX-3). Holders then pay up to 2% of the value each fill
trades, inside the μ band but against `README.md`'s "Competition fills earlier". All four were confirmed by a proof of
concept. Of the lows, thirteen are in the keeper, nine in the listing data and the trust statements, eight in the hub
and the factory, and two in the deploy scripts. Thirteen findings are contested, ten were already recorded, two were
refuted and seven are unverified. One contested finding needs a decision before the external audit: under
Glamsterdam's EIP-8037 the factory's creation transaction needs about 60.5M gas, above a 60M block (F-24).

Each surviving id is `RX-…`, the red team of 2026-10-18, a new id family that no test carries yet. The workflow's `F-`
id follows each in parentheses; an `F-` id of an earlier record is always named with its record. Ids run by severity,
then by evidence, then by `F-` id. The evidence is either *PoC confirmed* or *traced*, read only, with the code-truth
verdicts: *both confirm*; *one confirms, one partly*; *confirms*, where a finding claimed low got one code-truth check;
*both partly*; *partly*.

## RX-1: request fills trade at Hermes' last price, however old (F-01, medium, PoC confirmed)

**Claim.** The keeper's request fills (`fillMint`, `fillRedeem`) run no Pyth parse, and the executor's on-chain floor
counts only USDC, so the bot's prices decide what a fill pays. The request path prices every share and every leg at
Hermes' `latest` update and checks only that a price exists, never its publish time or confidence. Starts refuse a
feed quiet for 60 seconds, and auction fills take only an update for their own second; request fills have neither
rule. A leg with no pool is kept at full Pyth, so on nights and weekends a requester can sell the bot tokens after an
off-hours drop (a redeem request) or take its stock after a rise (a mint request), up to its float and its stock
allowance.

**Evidence.**

  `prices.every(Boolean)`. `:93` prices each leg from `hermes.latest`, and `:110-111` checks only `if (!p)`. Nothing
  in `requests.mjs` reads `publishTime` or `conf`.

  (`:75`), and only the auction path uses it.

  not copy it. The keeper's own end-to-end driver models a quiet feed as `latest` returning an update an hour old

## RX-2: share-priced Ondo stock valued at 1/sValue of its worth (F-02, medium, PoC confirmed)

## RX-3: μ keeps falling while a US-hours feed is shut (F-09, medium, PoC confirmed)

**Claim.** μ falls with time from 1.02 at eta to 0.98 at eta + 24 hours, whether or not a price can be read. A fill
needs every moving feed to publish in [block.timestamp − 12, block.timestamp − 1], and the repository records the six
Equity.US feeds (TSM, AVGO, LLY, JPM, WMT, V) as publishing only from 09:30 to 16:00 ET on trading days. An index
holding one of them is therefore first fillable at the next open, near μ 0.983, in steady state and with no attacker.
`startRebalance(0)` is permissionless and reads no price, while the keeper will not start while any feed has been
quiet for 60 seconds, so whoever moves first in a closed window owns the timing: a stranger who starts about 26 hours
before a reopen makes the auction's last block the only one that can price the stock, at μ 0.98 exactly.

**Evidence.**
- `src/WeightedHub.sol:795-801`: `_openMu` is `MU_MAX − mulDiv(0.04e18, now − eta, 86400)` (constants at `:100-104`)
  and reads no price. μ is 1.0 at 12 hours and 0.997 at 13.8 hours.
- `src/WeightedHub.sol:649-651`: `_buildSettlement` takes μ from `_openMu` before `_readPrices` parses. `_readPrices`
  (`:704-720`) calls `parsePriceFeedUpdatesUnique` for every moving id with `_priceTime()`, block.timestamp − 12
  (`:824-826`), as the window's start; a closed feed reverts the whole fill. Only a keep mask avoids it, and only the
  owner or guardian may pass one (`:517-521`).
- `script/PythIds.sol:17`, `:103-125` and `design/2026-10-05-token-admission-report.md:381-383` record the six

stock the fill delivers or keeps is re-sized. The only on-chain bound is the USDC floor, which counts no stock, so a
basket that now needs more of a poolless coin takes it from stock, within `stockAllowance`, unseen, while
`keeper/README.md:82-83` says such moves show at the floor.

**Evidence.**
- `keeper/src/KeeperExecutor.sol:214-221` and `:265-272`: `fillMint` takes `(id, shares, routes, stableRoute,
  minUsdcDelta, deadline)` and `fillRedeem` the same; neither has a nonce, a `maxAmountsIn` or a `maxSupply`, unlike
  `fill` (`:142-156`).
- `:243`: `_mintFor` calls `index.previewIssue(shares)` at execution. `:351-376`: `_acquire` takes min(balance,
  allowance) from stock first and buys the rest with an exact output whose `amountInMaximum` is the whole float
  (`:371`). `:228` and `:279`: the only check is the USDC delta against `minUsdcDelta`.
- `src/WeightedHub.sol:759` bumps `rebalanceNonce` on every settlement. The zap (`src/IndexZap.sol:35-36`, `:225-227`)
  and the builder's `buy` (`src/BasketBuilder.sol:33`, `:309`) revert `StaleRebalanceNonce` for exactly this reason.
  `src/MintRequests.sol:83-101`: `fill` checks only that the request is open, its deadline and its limit.
- `keeper/bot/requests.mjs:167-172` simulates at block L with an L + 1 override, and `:118-128` seeks a route only
  when the simulated need exceeds usable stock, so a leg the stock covers is sent with route `0x`.

**Attack path.** The keeper's `fillMint` for a request on index I sits in the mempool, priced on I's old basket, while
I's auction is open and the stock coin X is underweight. Another executor's fill of I's auction lands first, at any
μ. The keeper's fill re-reads `previewIssue`; with the turnover cap binding, X's units per share grow several times
over, the extra need comes out of stock, and fewer routed coins are bought, so the USDC floor is met. On the mechanism
verifier's example (Y routed at 90% of value, X from stock at 10%, weights 50/50), Y's units fall 20% and X's need
grows 2.76 times at μ 0.98, 2.80 at 1.0 and 2.84 at 1.02.

**Impact.** The keeper's stock moves off what it simulated and accepted. At the settlement's Pyth prices the value
change is ±(μ − 1) of the re-sized part, about ±0.36% of a share's value on that example and at most about 0.4%, but
the stock shift goes unseen either way, and combined with RX-2 the re-sized stock can be a share-priced leg valued at
1/sValue. The prior-knowledge verifier tightened the bound: extra need on a stock-covered leg comes out of stock only
up to the usable stock less the simulated need, and past that the fill reverts `NoRoute` mid-swaps. Index holders,
`MintRequests` escrows and protocol funds are untouched.

## Low survivors

Traced by reading; no proof of concept.

### RX-5: coarse units make fee rounding material (F-07, low)

**Mechanism.** Every fee is a whole number of wei-shares, rounded once per call: the gross up
(`src/WeightedHub.sol:377-380`), the exit fee down (`:390-392`), the platform's part down (`:395-398`, the creator
taking the remainder at `:491`) and the streaming fee down (`:449-450`), every accrual restarting the clock
(`:419-420`, XS-1's fix). The factory bounds units only to [1e4, (2^96 − 1) >> 10] raw per share, with no tie to the
token's decimals (`src/WeightedIndexFactory.sol:67-68`, `:346`), so a creator can make one wei-share worth tens of
thousands of dollars and one call's rounding worth up to about $394k.

**Impact.** On an index built coarse on purpose, the creator takes the platform's part, holders skip the exit fee, and
anyone can zero the streaming fee by accruing often. The loss falls on the fee recipients, chiefly the platform, up to
its whole streaming rate (at most 100 bps a year); XS-1's record called such rounding worthless, which it is not here.

**Fix.** Bound units relative to the token's decimals, the decimals-relative `MAX_UNITS` the leads proposed, so a
wei-share stays worth a negligible amount; the bound needs the owner's call.

### RX-6: five named guardians, not 13 of 19, can mint most wrapped copies (F-12, low)

**Mechanism.** Since April 2026 (Algorand on 2026-04-08; BNB Chain, Avalanche, Sui, Aptos and Sei on 2026-04-20)
Wormhole has run delegated guardian sets: `WormholeDelegatedGuardians` on Ethereum
(`0x1462800febd49232798132e8c8b721aa86c4c209`) names, per chain, the guardians that observe it, and the others sign
once a delegate quorum gossips a message. For chains 4 (BSC), 13 (Kaia), 15 (NEAR), 21 (Sui), 23 (Arbitrum) and 48
(Monad) that is 5 of 7; Solana and Base keep the full set.

**Impact.** For 11 of the 16 listed copies and 13 of the 31 queued ones, TRX and ZEC among them, five named guardians
can mint unbacked copies, whose reach is the whole of each leg. The documents give one bound, 13 of 19 (`README.md:446`,
`ARCHITECTURE.md:522`, the trust table at `design/2026-10-10-one-index-wormhole-legs.md:167`,
`design/2026-10-14-mainnet-engine-research.md:163`, `:406`). Only the power to mint drops to 5 of 7; upgrading the
copies' code still takes a governance message.

**Fix.** State the delegated sets per chain in the trust table, `README.md` and `ARCHITECTURE.md`, have the app show
each copy's quorum, and weigh it in the remaining attestations.

### RX-7: a crafted index sells a split Ondo leg at 1/k (F-15, low)

**Mechanism.** The factory reads no price, so weights and units are independent at creation (`_validateComponents`,
`src/WeightedIndexFactory.sol:336-359`), while the split analysis of 2026-10-18
(`design/2026-10-18-pre-mainnet-readings.md:90-93`) assumes a split leg turns underweight. A creator can give a
share-priced Ondo leg 1 bp of weight and units worth half the index; after a k:1 split the hub values it at 1/k, it is
still overweight, and every fill sells up to 20% of its units at 1/k of their worth (`src/WeightedMath.sol:128-162`).
A keep mask must leave two legs moving (`src/WeightedHub.sol:513`), so in a two-leg index the guardian cannot keep it.

**Impact.** Only holders of the crafted index lose, up to `MAX_TURNOVER` of the leg's units per fill at 1/k of their
worth; the numbers verifier's first fill takes 3.40 WETH ($10,200) for 58.82 AVGOon worth $100k. The premise is
visible on chain, and the loss sits inside the documented wrong-price bound.

**Fix.** Write into CF-3's procedure that the guardian cancels each auction of such an index around a split where it
cannot keep the leg, and have the app flag an index whose units and weights disagree at creation. Relisting the leg at
its token's own feed ends it.

### RX-8: after an issuer burn, issue keeps selling into the hole (F-16, low)

**Mechanism.** `_issue` reads each leg's balance only to measure the pull (`src/WeightedHub.sol:283-291`) and never
checks that the index already holds the existing supply's backing plus `totalClaims`, while redeem (`:360-368`) and
claim (`:316`) pay from whatever balance is there. After an issuer burns the index's balance (Ondo's `BURNER_ROLE`
needs no allowance), units and claims stay as they were, and the previews keep reporting full backing.

**Impact.** Each later mint refills the hole and earlier holders redeem it, so anyone who mints after the burn,
directly, through the zap, the builder or a request, loses up to the leg's weight times the deposit. The 2026-10-16
audit (its F-07, Already recorded) noted that issue stays open into the hole; who pays for it is not recorded.

**Fix.** A balance check on issue, which only verifies, as the design allows: refuse an issue while a leg's balance is
below the existing supply's backing plus its claims.

### RX-9: request fills verify no price (F-18, low)

**Mechanism.** `fillRedeem` and `fillMint` never call the hub's parse (`keeper/src/KeeperExecutor.sol:214-306`), and
the floor counts only USDC. For a coin with no pool, the only valuation is Hermes' unsigned `parsed` JSON from
`/v2/updates/price/latest` (`keeper/bot/hermes.mjs:86-94`), which pre-filters requests (`keeper/bot/math.mjs:187-191`)
and books kept stock at that price, and the floor's decision term inherits it (`keeper/bot/requests.mjs:131-134`).

**Impact.** A compromised Hermes, or anyone between the bot and Hermes, can inflate a poolless coin's price and make
the bot pay its whole float for one redeem request. The 2026-10-16 audit's F-39 (Contested) named the mechanism and
judged it unexploitable, a reason that does not hold on the request path.

**Fix.** Verify the signed update the bot already receives, for example by parsing it through Pyth's contract in the
simulation, or cross-check Hermes against a second source; and bound the USDC one request fill may spend (RX-1).

### RX-10: cheap pool pushes make the keeper pay whole reverted fills (F-21, low)

**Mechanism.** Request fills use the auctions' floor (`keeper/bot/requests.mjs:182-190`), and the requester's limit
sets how much room sits above it. XS-6 made a cancel or a gate change revert before any swap, but a pool push shows
only at the final `TooCostly` check (`keeper/src/KeeperExecutor.sol:228`), after `_acquire` has bought the whole
basket; the per-request back-off (`keeper/bot/backoff.mjs:14-19`) resets with every new request id (`requests.mjs:209`,
`:243`).

**Impact.** A requester with a tight limit pushes a pool ahead of each fill for about $1-4 and makes the keeper pay a
whole reverted fill, about 1-2M gas for ten real V3 legs, again with each new request id, until the hot key's ether is
gone. The requester's escrow (eight $10k requests are $80k) is refundable.

**Fix.** Bound each routed leg's spend in the fill's calldata, so a pushed pool reverts at its own leg rather than
after the whole basket, and back off per requester and per index rather than per request id.

### RX-11: the attestation procedure never screens the home-chain source (F-23, low)

**Mechanism.** Step four of the attestation procedure (`design/2026-10-10-one-index-wormhole-legs.md:140-146`) runs
`CheckToken` on the Ethereum copy, adds it at the coin's feed and pins (chain, source) in
`testFork_theWormholeCopiesAreTheBridges`. `CheckToken` reads only the Ethereum token's code and proxy slots
(`script/CheckToken.s.sol:30-45`, `:510-530`), and every copy is the same `BridgeToken` beacon, so the source is never
screened. FIL's queued source is Binance-Peg FIL, the proxy under one externally owned admin key that got DOGE
delisted on 2026-10-14.

**Impact.** That key can replace the source's code and mint unbacked FIL, which bridges into an unbacked copy priced
as FIL. The loss is the FIL leg of each index that holds it, about 1% of an equal-weighted hundred-coin index.

**Fix.** Add a home-chain source reading to step four, applying the DOGE rule, and hold FIL's attestation until its
source passes it.

### RX-12: a Tether-blocked index keeps receiving XAUT (F-06, low)

**Mechanism.** XAUT's live implementation behind `0x68749665…` (TetherToken `0x4C0d2c74…`, Sourcify full match) checks
`onlyNotBlocked` on the sender in `transfer` and on the sender and `_sender` in `transferFrom`, never the recipient.
Once Tether blocks the index, its XAUT cannot leave (redeem, claims and any fill where XAUT shrinks revert, and
`destroyBlockedFunds` can burn it), yet a fill delivers XAUT by the executor's own transfer and the settlement accepts
it on balances alone (`_checkBacking`, `src/WeightedHub.sol:772-791`). The verifiers made no RPC call; they relied on
that source and the repository's model of Tether-pattern tokens.

**Impact.** Every maskless fill that grows the XAUT leg moves good legs' value into a leg nobody can take out, up to
`MAX_TURNOVER` of each per fill, and the keeper starts exactly those. Holders who stay lose only if Tether destroys
the XAUT or never lifts the block. The 2026-10-16 audit (its F-51) bounded a frozen leg at a 26-hour stall while the
guardian sleeps; Tether's sender-only rule turns the stall into a transfer.

**Fix.** A probe in the hub that a growing leg can still leave the index (the leads' proposal, awaiting the owner's
choice); meanwhile the guardian keeps a blocked XAUT leg.

### RX-13: one block's pool state serves a whole auction or request (F-08, low)

**Mechanism.** `best()` returns a cached non-null route without re-quoting, and a null one for up to `REQUOTE_BLOCKS`
(25) while the amount does not grow (`keeper/bot/routes.mjs:70`). A route lives as long as its key, an auction's for
24 hours and a request's for up to 30 days, and nothing re-searches after a simulation reverts or turns unprofitable,
or after an on-chain revert; a kept coin's XS-5 quote is also one block's state and stands for 25 blocks.

**Impact.** One block of fake depth pins the keeper to a pool the attacker then empties, or pushes ahead of each fill
to force reverts, three of which evict it. Holders lose at most the documented lone-executor edge, 1 − `MU_MIN`, 2% of
the traded value per fill.

**Fix.** Re-quote the chosen route at each simulation, re-search after a failed or unprofitable simulation or a
revert, and refresh a kept coin's quote in each block that uses it.

### RX-14: one empty pool zeroes a poolless coin (F-14, low)

**Mechanism.** XS-5's fix stores `usdc: paths.length === 0 ? null : (pickUsdc ?? 0n)` (`keeper/bot/routes.mjs:83`),
so a coin with any pool that quotes nothing counts as zero, and `candidates()` tests a pool's existence only with
`UniswapV3Factory.getPool` (`:30-48`), which anyone makes non-zero with `createPool`. An uninitialized or empty pool
quotes nothing, and the path list is cached for good (`:24`).

**Impact.** One empty pool for an Ondo stock, XAUT or a Wormhole copy makes the keeper value every unit of it at zero,
so it refuses every auction where that leg shrinks and every redeem request holding it. No protocol, holder or float
funds are at risk, since the bot only grows more conservative; the numbers verifier found the timing and reach
narrower than claimed.

**Fix.** Count a pool as a candidate only when it is initialized and holds liquidity, and re-read candidates on a
schedule, which also fixes RX-17.

### RX-15: an issuer's mint key reaches the whole index (F-17, low)

**Mechanism.** `WeightedMath._targets` limits `share` by the shrinking legs alone (`src/WeightedMath.sol:153-156`), so
`MAX_TURNOVER` caps only what shrinking legs send out, and a growing leg's units rise by μ · share · (target −
current) with no cap (`_stepUnits`, `:95`). Anyone may start a maskless rebalance once the interval has passed
(`src/WeightedHub.sol:505-530`), and for a coin priced at its own market feed whose admin can mint (GALA, H, PLUME and
others), the issuer mints, sells and drives the feed down.

**Impact.** Each fill then sells up to 20% of every good leg to buy the diluted coin, once per interval, to holders
who stay through it. The trust statements give an issuer the reach of its own leg; it is the whole index, within the
unit cap. The 2026-10-13 audit's hardening note on the Wormhole trust row named the path and was not acted on.

**Fix.** A cap on a growing leg's unit growth per fill (the leads' proposal, needing the owner's call), or at least a
trust table that says an issuer able to mint reaches every leg, a fifth of each per fill.

### RX-16: a negative `MIN_PROFIT_USD` has no budget (F-19, low)

**Mechanism.** `MIN_PROFIT_USD` (`keeper/bot/config.mjs:74`) has no lower bound (`number()` defaults its minimum to
−Infinity, `:51-59`) and gates auction fills, starts and request fills alike. The floor's decision term is minProfit +
gas − inventory (`keeper/bot/math.mjs:130-134`), so with a negative setting each fill may lose up to its size, with no
cap per index, requester, day or in total; XS-7's pre-filter compares the limit with the escrow before fees, so a
zero-surplus request passes.

**Impact.** Only when the operator sets the value below zero: an attacker posts zero-surplus requests on its own
fee-charging index, collects the creator's part of each fee, and the keeper pays the subsidy once per block until the
float is gone. The records call a negative value a subsidy the operator chooses (`keeper/README.md:109`); none says
anyone can draw on it without limit.

**Fix.** Give the subsidy a budget per index, per requester and per day, or apply it to auctions only.

### RX-17: candidate pools are cached for the process's life (F-25, low)

**Mechanism.** `candidates()` caches, per token and for the process's life, the V3 paths whose pools existed at the
first read, an empty list included (`keeper/bot/routes.mjs:24`, `:30-48`); `forget` clears only `chosen`, and the bot
builds its routes object once (`keeper/keeper.mjs:39`). A token with no pool at its first lookup keeps `usdc: null`
(`routes.mjs:83`) and counts at full Pyth (`keeper/bot/math.mjs:57-58`) on every later auction and request.

**Impact.** XS-5's rule that a coin whose pools quote nothing counts as nothing never reaches a pool created later,
for instance by arbitrageurs after a Wormhole or Binance-Peg copy depegs, so until a restart the bot keeps paying full
Pyth for it out of the float.

**Fix.** Re-read candidate pools on a schedule, for example with each 25-block scan.

### RX-18: the Pyth update fee is outside the keeper's profit (F-28, low)

**Mechanism.** `priceFill` reads `getUpdateFee(sim.updates)` and sends it as `value` through `fill` to the hub, which
forwards it to Pyth (`keeper/keeper.mjs:175-181`, `:193-202`), but `gasUsd` counts gas only, and neither `profitUsd`
nor `usdcFloor` subtracts the fee (`keeper/bot/math.mjs:126`, `:130-134`). Starts send no fee (`keeper.mjs:355`).

**Impact.** Pyth's fee is zero today, but three of Pyth's five keys can set it at any time, and the bot would then send
fills that each lose up to the fee, unnoticed. Only the operator's hot-key ether is at risk.

**Fix.** Price the fee in dollars and subtract it in `profitUsd` and `usdcFloor`.

### RX-19: ZK is a bridge copy tiered Native (F-33, low)

**Mechanism.** `tierOf` puts ZK (`0x66A5cFB2e9c529f14FE6364Ad1075dF3a649C0A5`, `script/MainnetAddresses.sol:170-171`)
in the Native set (`script/PythIds.sol:252`), and the tier test counts it among the 49 Natives
(`test/ConfigureWeighted.t.sol:99-100`). The source reading of 2026-10-18
(`design/2026-10-18-token-source-reading.md:151`) found a beacon proxy whose only minter is ZKsync's L1 Native Token
Vault, and ZK is issued on ZKsync Era, whose L2NativeTokenVault held 20,131,693.86 ZK on 2026-10-18 against
19,871,600.70 ZK on Ethereum.

**Impact.** The label loses nothing itself, but the app would show ZK as Native while its holders depend on ZKsync's
bridge and governance; if those fail, each index loses up to its ZK leg.

**Fix.** Move `M.ZK` from the Native set to the Bridged set (`script/PythIds.sol:238-243`), set the tier test's counts
to 48 Natives and 21 Bridged, and name ZKsync's bridge as the party.

### RX-20: USX and CASH are stablecoins, and CASH's issuer can move balances (F-34, low)

**Mechanism.** Section 2a's to-attest table (`design/2026-10-10-one-index-wormhole-legs.md:129`, `:134`) and the
readings' list of 31 (`design/2026-10-18-pre-mainnet-readings.md:117-119`) include USX and CASH, whose Pyth feeds are

**Impact.** None today: nothing is listed, and the copies do not exist. Listed, they would add two stablecoin legs the
count excluded, and with CASH an issuer power the WBT rule refuses.

**Fix.** Remove USX and CASH from the queue.

### RX-21: fills near the gas cap run out of gas (F-44, low)

**Mechanism.** `priceFill` measures a fill's gas with an `eth_call` that sends no gas field
(`keeper/bot/chain.mjs:106`), so the node's default, often 50M or more, applies; it then sends min(1.2 · used, 2^24)
(`keeper/keeper.mjs:189-190`,
`keeper/bot/math.mjs:90`) without simulating at that limit. The whole settlement runs inside one CALL from the
executor to the hub, which keeps 1/64 of the gas back.

**Impact.** A fill within about 230k gas of the cap passes simulation and runs out of gas on chain, burning about
16.5M gas and a back-off strike, at most three per proposal. Only the operator's ether is at risk.

**Fix.** Simulate with the gas limit the bot will send, and skip a fill that does not fit it.

### RX-22: `creatorOf` can name anyone without consent (F-45, low)

**Mechanism.** `transferCreator` (`src/WeightedIndexFactory.sol:218-226`) is a one-step write that refuses only zero,
the factory and an index; the new creator never accepts. `README.md:157` and `:243-245` tell the app to beat
look-alikes by keying on the address and showing `creatorOf`.

**Impact.** A copycat index can display a well-known creator, who then also receives its fees unasked. There is no
theft, and every basket stays backed.

**Fix.** A two-step transfer the new creator accepts, so that `creatorOf` can vouch for the creator.

### RX-23: a chain-id override switches off the mainnet guardrails (F-50, low)

**Mechanism.** Every mainnet guardrail keys on `block.chainid == 1` (`DeployChecks.isPinnedChain`,
`script/DeployChecks.sol:189-191`): `checkOwner`'s contract, EIP-7702, threshold and module checks (`:63-78`), the
address-book defaults and `MainnetOverride` (`:232-241`), the role list (`:167-175`), and CF-5's `checkBuild`
(`script/DeployWeighted.s.sol:99`). In Foundry v1.8.1 a script runs with the chain-id override when one is set
(`--chain-id`, `FOUNDRY_CHAIN_ID` or `foundry.toml`'s `chain_id`), while the broadcast still signs for chain 1.

**Impact.** A mainnet deploy run with an override skips every pin, so an externally owned owner, a Safe with a module,
environment market addresses and an unreleased build all pass. Nothing is lost directly, since the deploy happens
once, before any funds; the exploitability verifier found the likelihood and cost overstated.

**Fix.** Key the pins on a fact an override cannot change, such as the code at the address book's addresses, refuse to
run when an override is set, and add the check to the deploy runbook.

### RX-24: decoys hold the keeper's check slots (F-03, low)

**Mechanism.** CF-7 and XS-7 bound the bot's work to the eight richest open auctions (`keeper/keeper.mjs:256-261`) and
the largest requests per side (`keeper/bot/math.mjs:171-200`), but `worthChecking` applies the cap before the back-off
filter (`keeper.mjs:416-421`), so a backed-off auction keeps its slot. Only simulations that revert and checks that
throw are backed off (`keeper/bot/requests.mjs:174`, `:217`, `:223`), so a request that simulates cleanly but pays
under `MIN_PROFIT_USD`, needs restock or has no preview is chosen again every block for up to 30 days.

**Impact.** No funds are lost; while decoys hold the slots, the first-party filler stops serving the smaller auctions
and requests behind them. The numbers verifier found the auction half firm and the request decoys weaker than claimed,
on cost and stability.

**Fix.** Apply the back-off before the cap, and back off a skip that repeats, not only a revert.

### RX-25: `PYTH` cannot be repointed (F-04, low)

**Mechanism.** `PYTH` is an immutable of the one `WeightedHub` implementation every clone delegates to
(`src/WeightedHub.sol:112`, `:190`), price ids are copied at initialize, and every settlement must parse at `PYTH`
(`:707`); on chain 1 the deploy accepts only `0x4305FB66…` (`script/MainnetAddresses.sol:283`). Pyth's contract-address
page, read on 2026-10-18, lists `0x14b9` as Ethereum's Core while the readings say it lists nothing; both proxies are
live, and Pyth's DAO upgraded `0x4305` in place on 2026-08-26.

**Impact.** Nothing is lost or frozen, since issue, redeem, `redeemSkipping` and claims read no price; but if `0x4305`
stops verifying updates, rebalancing ends for good on every index.

**Fix.** Get Pyth's written confirmation of which address stays served, already a pre-mainnet item; record the
dependency among the known limitations; and decide before the external audit whether a migration path is worth a
design change.

### RX-26: three induced reverts evict the keeper (F-05, low)

**Mechanism.** After `MAX_REVERTED_FILLS` (3) on-chain reverts for one index and proposal, `Backoff.blocked()` stays
true until the auction closes (`keeper/bot/backoff.mjs:5-11`, `keeper/keeper.mjs:373-374`), whatever caused them. An
issue of a little over 0.1% of supply bundled ahead of each fill trips `SupplyMovedOn` against `maxSupply`, (supply +
pending fee) · 1.001 (`keeper/src/KeeperExecutor.sol:155`, `:431-434`), on all three strikes.

**Impact.** Holders lose the difference between the μ the keeper would have filled at and about 0.98, times that
rebalance's traded value, inside documented bounds. The eviction is XS-4's chosen fix; the prior-knowledge verifier
found strike sources no record covers.

**Fix.** Let a strike lapse after some blocks instead of lasting the whole auction, so a front-run issue cannot evict
the keeper for the rest of it.

### RX-27: a seconds-long push on thin venues prices aligned fills (F-10, low)

**Mechanism.** A fill settles at the one Pyth update first published at or after block.timestamp − 12
(`src/WeightedHub.sol:691-722`, `:824-826`), refused only when its confidence exceeds 2% of the price (`:738`), and the
price is never compared with `emaPrice` (`:721`). The five Crypto.*ON legs (NVDAON, AAPLON, GOOGLON, MSFTON, TSLAON)
are aggregated from thin Gate and MEXC books, and feeds such as H/USD from a few venues.

**Impact.** A push lasting seconds on those books at the settlement second, cheapest on a weekend, moves the price
every aligned index's fill uses, inside the 2% bound, so a leg priced e off moves e times its traded value to the
executor, across many indexes in one block. It is a bounded leak inside V5-M2's unit cap, and the numbers verifier
found the launch-scale example overstated about 2.1 times.

**Fix.** An EMA-deviation bound in the settlement, refusing a price too far from the same update's EMA (the leads'
proposal, needing the owner's call); it reads nothing the parse does not already return.

### RX-28: BTT's bridge has closed (F-11, low)

**Impact.** No loss today, since the peg holds within about 1% and nothing is deployed. After a depeg, holders of an
index that chose BTT lose on the fills that trade it at the native price. The repository records that the same bridge
closed, and two owner rulings on closed bridges were never applied to BTT.

**Fix.** Put BTT to the owner under those rulings, and delist it if they apply.

### RX-29: Ondo's termination is a loss, not a stall (F-13, low)

**Mechanism.** The readings of 2026-10-18 conclude that CF-3's burn and mint harms "do not come from Ondo's corporate
actions, since balances never change" (`design/2026-10-18-pre-mainnet-readings.md:65`, `:79`, `:103`;

least 30 business days' notice and pay only KYC'd redeemers, and the readings themselves say acquisitions are

**Impact.** Holders still in after a termination lose the terminated leg's weight: the hub moves only component

**Fix.** Add the terminal actions to CF-3, the readings and the guardian's procedure, and have the app tell holders of

### RX-30: one id Hermes does not serve stalls every index (F-20, low)

**Mechanism.** `worthChecking`, `startChecks` and `scan` each make one batched `hermes.latest` call over every
component id of the indexes they look at (`keeper/keeper.mjs:246-248`, `:329-331`; `keeper/bot/requests.mjs:76-78`),

**Impact.** One listed id Hermes stops serving, in any index with an open auction or request, which anyone can
create, stalls fills, starts and request fills for every index the keeper serves; only its availability is at risk.
The mechanism verifier found the request variant fails at one step.

**Fix.** Send `ignore_invalid_price_ids`, or batch per index so that one bad id fails only its own index.

### RX-31: the documented feed sessions disagree with Pyth's metadata (F-26, low)

### RX-32: the SEI copy's home side is shutting down (F-35, low)

**Mechanism.** The SEI copy (`0xf91e605af079384cc7077B3914A4a36019a89EE8`, `script/MainnetAddresses.sol:240-241`) wraps

### RX-33: `PYTH` has no independent pin (F-41, low)

**Mechanism.** CR-L08's hand-written table (`test/ConfigureWeighted.t.sol:149`) holds only the 93 token addresses and
price ids; `PYTH` and the market addresses exist only in `script/MainnetAddresses.sol` (`:246-283`), and the unit tests

**Impact.** For `PYTH` alone (the claims about the router and the PoolManager failed, and for USDC, USDT and the

**Fix.** Pin `PYTH` in the hand-written table as CR-L08 pins the tokens, and give the fork check something a look-alike
cannot copy.

### RX-34: start checks block the keeper's main loop (F-43, low)

**Mechanism.** `routes.forget` drops the start keys every block (`keeper/keeper.mjs:412-413`), so each start check
re-quotes up to seven paths per moving leg, one `eth_call` at a time, for each ready index in turn, and only a start
that pays ends the loop (`:305-360`, `:140-168`). `onBlock` awaits all of it, so fills, replacements and request checks
wait behind it.

**Impact.** With many ready indexes, which anyone can create for about $1,000 of recoverable capital each, the main
loop stalls for several blocks every 25. No index funds are at risk. CF-7's record said the start path was already
safe, and its fix covered the fill path.

**Fix.** Keep start routes between blocks, quote in parallel, and bound the start checks per block as CF-7 bounded the
fill path.

### RX-35: BP is an exchange's token (F-52, low)

**Mechanism.** BP is queued for attestation (`design/2026-10-10-one-index-wormhole-legs.md:125`), while the listing
rules refuse exchange tokens ("OKB, BGB, GT; HTX falls under the same rule", `:56-57`; `README.md:419-420`). CoinGecko,
CoinMarketCap (id 39686, the `cmc_id` of Pyth's Crypto.BP/USD) and GeckoTerminal tag it as a centralized exchange's
token. The mechanism verifier found two of the finding's steps do not hold as stated: that one live key holds both
its mint and freeze authority, and that the rules as written refuse BP.

**Impact.** Nothing today: BP is a row in two design records. The count of 2026-10-10 says it applied the exchange-token
rule to the queue, and BP passed through it.

**Fix.** Put BP to the owner under the exchange-token rule, and drop it from the queue if the rule applies.

### RX-36: the keeper cannot see EIP-8037 state gas (F-56, low)

**Mechanism.** Under EIP-8037 the GAS opcode returns only the gas left, state gas is drawn first from a reservoir
above 2^24, and `tx.gas` may go up to 2^32 − 1. The executor reports `gasStart − gasleft()`
(`keeper/src/KeeperExecutor.sol:151`, `:162`, `:222`, `:229`, `:273`, `:280`), and the bot's `eth_call` sends no gas
field, so new-slot state gas stays out of what it measures; it then sends min(1.2 · used, 2^24).

**Impact.** After Glamsterdam, request fills that write new slots, such as a new index's first mint, run out of gas,
up to three per request, and valid fills above 2^24 are skipped. Only the operator's gas is lost.

**Fix.** Before Glamsterdam reaches mainnet, measure the transaction's whole gas with an explicit gas field, and allow
`tx.gas` above 2^24 where EIP-8037 does.
