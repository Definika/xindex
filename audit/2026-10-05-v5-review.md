# Internal review of v5's weighted index (2026-10-05)

One reviewer read the uncommitted v5 surface on branch `v5-custom-weights`, without changing it:
- `WeightedHub` and `WeightedIndexFactory`, their interfaces, and the vendored Pyth interface;
- the tests and mocks;
- `design/2026-10-05-custom-weights.md`;
- for comparison, `IndexHub`, `IndexFactory` and v3's oracle findings (L-06, DA-05).

It checked the hub's use of Pyth against Pyth's source at the vendored commit `2bb0eb54`. It ran six proofs of concept
in a separate Foundry project that imported `src/`.

**Result.** No way to take more than the documented bounds from an index whose prices are honest, but:
- 2 medium findings: a family of indexes that could never rebalance, and a cap measured in the prices it guards
  against;
- 3 low;
- 4 informational.

Each finding's id is `V5-…`, and a test for a fixed finding is named `test_V5M1_...`, as `AGENTS.md` gate 6 requires.
The fixes were checked against 21 mutants of the hub and the factory: each fix removed, and each rounding flipped.
Every one fails a test.

| Id | Severity | Finding | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| V5-M1 | Medium | Two components with the same Pyth price id, such as two wrappers of one coin listed on one feed, could never be rebalanced. Pyth's `_parseSingleMerkleUpdate` fills only the first requested slot with an id, so the parse reverts whatever the executor brings. The test mock resolved each slot on its own and hid it. | Fixed: the factory refuses a second component on an id an earlier one carries (`DuplicatePriceId`), and the hub's initializer refuses one too. The mock now fills slots as Pyth does. `test_V5M1_twoComponentsOnOneFeedAreRefused`, `test_V5M1_initializeRefusesTwoComponentsOnOneFeed`, `test_V5M1_pythNeverFillsOneIdTwice`. |
| V5-M2 | Medium | The turnover cap, 20% of the moving value, was measured in the reported prices it was meant to guard against. On an index far from its weights, two wrong feeds took 89% of it in one fill: A and B truly $1, weights 1% and 99%, units 99 A and 1 B; A reported at $0.0333 and B at $30 sent out 89 A a token for 0.097 B. One feed ten times low took about 20%. | Fixed: the cap counts units. A fill sends out at most `MAX_TURNOVER` (20%) of each component's own units, whatever any price says, by moving every leg the same smaller share of the way. `test_V5M2_theTurnoverCapCountsUnits`, `test_V5M2_wrongPricesTakeAtMostAFifthOfEachLeg`, `testFuzz_V5M2_noPriceTakesMoreThanAFifthOfALeg`, and the invariant suite's `turnoverBroken`. A broken or malicious feed set can still take up to a fifth of each component sold, once a week; that is the trust in Pyth the app states. |
| V5-L1 | Low | The prices were pinned to the start of the previous minute, known up to two minutes before the last block that could use them. An executor could watch the market that long and fill only when the gap favoured it, which the design called "no choice". | Fixed: the prices are one slot back, `PRICE_LAG` (12 seconds) before the block, published in the window up to the second before it. The executor still chooses whether to fill in a block, seeing its price a slot ahead; that option is stated in the hub's NatSpec and the spec. `test_V5L1_theHubAsksPythForTheLastSlot`, `test_V5L1_priceTimeIsOneSlotBack`. |
| V5-L2 | Low | A fill that traded nothing still restarted `MIN_REBALANCE_INTERVAL`, which v4's `BandTooNarrow` had prevented. With one moving component the target is exactly the units, so the guardian could keep the rest and stall rebalancing at no cost, past its own replacement (RT-07's case, cheaper). | Fixed: an index needs two components (`MIN_COMPONENTS`), a keep mask must leave two moving, and a fill that would release less than `MIN_TRADE` (0.1%) of the moving value reverts `NothingToRebalance`. `test_V5L2_aKeepMaskMustLeaveTwoMoving`, `test_V5L2_aFillThatTradesNothingCannotRestartTheInterval`, `test_aSingleComponentIsRefused`. |
| V5-L3 | Low | Units grow as a component's relative price falls, and fixed weights keep buying it, so a coin that collapses about a thousand times from units near `MAX_UNITS` asks for more than `uint96` holds, and every fill reverts until the guardian keeps it. | Documented as a known limitation in the hub and the spec; the fill stops rather than wraps. `test_V5L3_unitsBeyondUint96StopTheFill`. |
| V5-I1 | Info | `PriceFeed` does not return `prevPublishTime`, so the hub can check the id and the window Pyth returns but not that the update was the first: uniqueness is Pyth's to keep, and Pyth's DAO upgrades its contract. | Documented in the hub's NatSpec and the spec's trust section. |
| V5-I2 | Info | The design note was stale against the code: it described a third template on `HybridIndexFactory`, `proposePriceId` and `previewRebalanceAt`. | Fixed by documentation: the note was rewritten for what was built. |
| V5-I3 | Info | `AGENTS.md` still said "No price in any contract" without the exception. | Fixed by documentation: the rule names v4's contracts and points to the v5 section. |
| V5-I4 | Info | The scan for price reads named only `IPyth` and `parsePriceFeedUpdates`, so a read through `getPriceUnsafe` or a renamed interface would have passed. | Fixed: the scan also names Pyth's getters, its pushes and its price struct. `test_V5I4_onlyTheWeightedHubReadsAPrice`. |

**Test gaps the reviewer named, and what was done:**
- Pyth's semantics were tested only against a mock written from the same understanding. The mock now follows Pyth's
  fill loop. A fork test settling a rebalance on a real signed update still needs a Pyth API key: Hermes and its
  Benchmarks history answer 401 without one since Pyth's upgrade of 2026-08-26.
- Nothing valued a fill at prices other than the settling ones. `testFuzz_V5M2_noPriceTakesMoreThanAFifthOfALeg`
  now does, over prices from one wei to `MAX_PRICE` and units far from the weights.
- `test_aHundredComponentsSettle`, since folded into `test_aHundredComponentsFitOneTransaction`, excludes Pyth's real parse cost (signatures, Merkle proofs, calldata). It is still
  unmeasured, and needs the same key.
- Duplicate ids and the no-op stall now have tests (V5-M1, V5-L2).

**Checked and sound,** as the reviewer reported:
- the rounding, SA-07's way, and that units never reach zero;
- the μ bound and the cap at the settling prices;
- overflow in the price scaling and the value sums;
- the Pyth call: ids and window rechecked, all of `msg.value` forwarded, no ether retained, reentry blocked;
- exits never call Pyth;
- the protections carried from v4: DA-14, DA-13, VR-04, the MU_MAX fallback (R14, XA-27), CR-I01 and RT-21;
- the factory's salt, guardian logic, relisting, decimals frozen at proposal and initializer;
- `IndexZap` and `MintRequests` against the weighted factory.
