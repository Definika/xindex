| DA-01 | HIGH | `_clip` floors a target and the dropped value is paid to the executor | FIXED | `IndexRule._clip` rounds the share into units with `Math.Rounding.Ceil`, so the target basket is never worth less than the basket it replaces. The ceiling still holds: in the interior branch `share <= floor(hi*p/scale)`, so `ceil(share*scale/p) <= hi` because `hi` is a whole number; `MAX_TARGET_UNITS`, the `SafeCast.toUint96` in `startRebalance` and the `1.5 * MAX_TARGET_UNITS == type(uint96).max` headroom are unaffected, and the `Math.max(..., lo[i])` is kept for the WAD-scale case (LR-09). Reproduced before the fix with a do-nothing executor keeping 15.998e18 of a component against `amountsIn == [0, 0]`. |
| DA-02 | HIGH | A quiet pool makes the snapshot TWAP a spot read | FIXED | New `IndexRule.MAX_OBSERVATION_AGE = TWAP_WINDOW / TWAP_SUB_WINDOWS` (10 minutes) and error `StalePriceHistory(pool, age)`. `_snapshotPrice` reads the newest observation through the new `IUniswapV3Pool.observations` and refuses a window Uniswap would extrapolate from the pool's current tick and current liquidity. Test: `test_DA02_aSnapshotRefusesAPoolThatHasNotTraded`, which also pins the last acceptable age so the bound is not off by one. The likelihood term, how often the thin default pools actually go 1,801 seconds without an observation, still needs an archive node; see the report. |
| DA-03 | MEDIUM | `MAX_TICK_DEVIATION` does not bound a sustained manipulation | DOCUMENTED | No in-window statistic can detect a uniform shift: the check compares the three sub-window means only with each other, so a manipulation held across the whole window moves all three equally and reads spread 0. Comparing each part against the window mean, which the round-one finder proposed, is mathematically the same thing for a uniform shift and was rejected for that reason. The documents now say what the check is (an outlier filter for a short spike) and what actually bounds a sustained push (the `minLiquidity` floor and the cost of holding a pool off-market for thirty minutes). The real bound it does give is recorded: with spread at most 2,000 a single distorted part moves the window mean by at most a third of its own shift, about 667 ticks or 6.9%. |
| DA-04 | MEDIUM | One shallow block blocks `startRebalance` for 30 minutes | DOCUMENTED | The fix was built and rejected. Measuring each part's harmonic liquidity separately and taking the median does shake off the griefer, and the whole suite passed with it, but it also shakes off a real push through an empty range confined to one part, which is exactly the attack XA-01 exists to stop: `test_XA01_aSnapshotThroughAnEmptyRangeCannotStart` began failing with `PriceHistoryDeviates` instead of `ThinPriceHistory`, and that test and `test_XA01_aOneBlockOutlierThroughLiquidityCannotStart` are deliberately two distinct defences. Trading a value protection for a liveness annoyance is the wrong way round, so the whole-window harmonic mean stays and its cost is written down, in `_observe` and in the docs. The grief is recoverable: anyone may retry, and the griefer must pay again every thirty minutes for as long as they want the index frozen. |
| DA-05 | MEDIUM | The auction band bounds the multiplier, not the price drift | DOCUMENTED | Shortening the notice or repricing the auction is a product decision, not a bug fix, and `AGENTS.md` requires such a change to be raised explicitly rather than made in passing. The documents now state the exposure correctly: settlement is a fixed-rate trade against a snapshot up to three days old, the 1.5-to-0.9 band bounds the multiplier on the bought legs only, and whatever the market moved over the notice window is on top and is not bounded. The README sentence that put the loss at "up to 10% of the bought value" is corrected. |
| DA-06 | MEDIUM | A kept component's unchecked price reshapes every trading target | FIXED | `_snapshotMarket` now prices a kept component with `_snapshotPrice` under the capped methodology too, so the cap that sits in the denominator of every other component's weight comes from a checked price. This narrows XA-05, which is recorded in the code, the interface NatSpec and the docs: under the capped methodology the keep mask routes around a component that cannot be transferred, not around one whose pool cannot be read. Tests: `test_DA06_aKeptComponentIsSnapshotCheckedUnderCapped`, and `test_XA05_keptComponentsNeedNoSnapshotPrice` renamed `...UnderUncapped` for the half of its claim that survives. |
| DA-07 | MEDIUM | A dead pool locks a token out of every config | DOCUMENTED | A pool with no in-range liquidity has no meaningful price, so refusing to configure against it is the conservative and correct behaviour; the trap is that it is not written down. ## What a reviewer should look at hardest

1. **DA-02's threshold.** Ten minutes is one sub-window, which is the principled choice, but it is a product-visible
   liveness cost: a component whose pool is silent for longer cannot be snapshotted until it trades. Several default
   pools are thin. If that proves too strict in practice the constant is the only thing to change, and the keep mask
   and a retry are the escapes meanwhile.
2. **DA-06's narrowing of XA-05.** A keep mask under the capped methodology no longer rescues a component whose pool
   cannot be read. That was XA-05's stated purpose, and half of it is now gone on purpose.
3. **DA-13's new entry point.** It widens the token's surface for an availability problem that never put value at risk.
   It is the one change here that is a feature rather than a repair.
4. **DA-04 and DA-11**, where a working fix was written and then thrown away. Both rejections rest on the claim that
   the proposed fix would weaken a defence more than it helps. If that reasoning is wrong, both should be revisited.
