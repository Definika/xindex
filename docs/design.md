# Design decisions

| Topic | Decision |
|---|---|
| Chain | Three deployments that share nothing, on Ethereum, on BNB Chain since 2026-11-13 ([`design/2026-11-13-bnb-chain-deployment.md`](../design/2026-11-13-bnb-chain-deployment.md)) and on Base since 2026-11-21 ([`design/2026-11-21-base-deployment.md`](../design/2026-11-21-base-deployment.md)); an index lives on one chain and holds that chain's tokens in kind. Nothing crosses a chain inside a protocol contract: on Ethereum, coins from other chains are held as the Ethereum tokens that represent them; on BNB Chain, nine of the twelve listed coins are Binance-Peg tokens; on Base, thirteen of the sixteen are copies Base's own bridge mints. The keeper's executor, which holds no role, calls Wormhole's Token Bridge to restock them. |
| Composition | 2 to 200 components per index (100 until 2026-11-21), fixed at creation, with their weights (basis points summing to 10,000). Since 2026-11-23 the creator may change the weights, never the coins, behind a notice of 7 days (`proposeWeights`, `executeWeights`), and the owner or guardian may cancel a change. A new list is a new index. |
| Creation | Only a wallet the factory's owner approved creates an index (`setApprovedCreator`, at once; `CreatorNotApproved` otherwise). `createAndIssue(name, symbol, components, params, shares, to)` creates the index and issues its first shares in one call, the basket pulled from the creator, who approved the index's address (`predictIndexAddress`) before it existed; `createIndex(name, symbol, components, params)` creates one with no shares, for an index too wide for one transaction. Each takes one `ComponentSpec {token, weight, units}` per component. The app derives the starting units from the weights at Pyth's prices; each lies between `MIN_RAW_UNITS` and `maxUnitsOf(token)`, and on a factory with an oracle each component's share of the index's value at Pyth's prices lies within 5% of its weight, or the creation reverts `OffWeights`: on units alone an index created off its weights loses that distance to whoever fills its auctions, 15.5% of a 50/50 index created 90/10 by value in one fill. The approval is read at creation and nowhere else: it reaches no existing index and gives no power over funds, and an index's creator role still moves to any account, which is how a partner takes over an index the team made. The creator may change the weights of the coins the index holds behind 7 days of notice (`proposeWeights`); the coins never change. A listing records only the token's decimals, which bound the units an index may start with. |
| Rebalancing | Once the index's interval (7 to 91 days, chosen at creation) has passed, anyone starts a rebalance, a plain call. After 2 hours of notice, a 4-hour auction lets any contract settle it: the executor names every component's new units, and the index accepts them when each leg keeps at least four fifths and at most twice its units and a weighted gain in units clears the auction's bar, which starts at a half and halves every twelve minutes. An accepted fill never lowers the weighted product of the units, so at any prices the index is worth at least `e^(bar − D)` times what it was, `D` its distance from its weights. The owner or guardian may start with a quote instead, `startRebalance(keepMask, prices, tolerance)`: a fill of that proposal must clear the bar and also bring in, at those prices, at least `1 − tolerance` of the value it takes out, so an executor alone at the auction's end keeps at most the tolerance of what it trades. A stranger's plain start carries no quote; the guardian replaces it within its six hours. |
| Prices | No issue, redeem or claim reads one. A rebalance fill is judged on units first, and on a factory with an oracle is then checked against Pyth's prices of the last minute through `PythPrices`: it must bring in at least what it takes, less a tolerance that rises from nothing to 2% over the auction. That check can only refuse: a price that is stale, missing or unusable stops a fill that moves the coin, and one that is wrong or hostile can do no more than pass a fill the units already accept. Creation uses the same reader to hold an index to its weights. The owner sets each token's feed behind the listing delay, and the guardian can cancel one. The other place a price enters is the quote the owner or guardian may post with a start: their own figures, fixed at the start, used only to refuse a fill the bar accepts; one that refuses every trade worth making still passes a fill that shrinks no leg, so its poster can settle a sliver, which restarts the index's interval for strangers, and the owner or guardian may start again a week later (RW-3). A lone executor keeps at most the tolerance of what it trades; on units alone it would take the index's distance from its weights out of the holders' value (0.3 to 0.6% in an ordinary week, 5.0% after one coin of two doubles). The app values an index off chain, and the keeper prices its own trades off chain and posts Pyth's update with each fill. |
| Swaps | Uniswap's Universal Router 2.0.0, V3 or V4 pools, and the chain's own DEX (PancakeSwap's V3 and V2 pools on BNB Chain, Aero's CL and basic pools on Base, since 2026-11-26), one venue per leg, chosen by the caller. The zap fits about 35 components in one transaction (EIP-7825's 16.78M gas cap); the basket builder covers wider baskets over several. |
| Fees | Paid in shares, never in components. The creator's rate (0 to 1%) plus the platform's (0 to 0.25%, the policy when the index was created) on every issue and redeem; a referrer named at mint takes a share of the platform's part. An optional streaming fee (creator up to 5% a year, platform up to 1%) accrues in shares and moves no tokens. |
| Access | A gated index issues only to its creator and the recipients its creator allows, until the creator opens it for good; a referrer it would refuse gets no share, which stays the platform's. An unlisted index is simply not shown by the app. |
| Assets | Ethereum ERC-20s: the issuer's own contract where Ethereum has one, Bitcoin as WBTC, and otherwise the copy Wormhole's token bridge wraps from the coin's home chain. Tether Gold and Ondo's tokenized stocks are listed too. The labels the app must show (Native, Bridged, Custodial, issuer powers) are in [assets.md](assets.md). |
| Stablecoins | Mint with USDC or USDT through the zap, the basket builder, or a limit order in `MintRequests`. Burn to USDC or USDT, or in kind. The two stablecoins are the only currencies, on each chain its own, and no contract takes ether (the owner's call of 2026-11-18). Index tokens are fungible, so the protocol does not track which stablecoin a holder used. |
| Governance | No admin can move an index's funds, mint or burn its shares, pause or upgrade it, or change its units or weights. The owner, a Safe that needs at least 3 signatures, proposes listings (effective after 2 days) and guardian changes (after 7), and sets the fee policy for new indexes. The guardian can cancel pending listings, delist, cancel a rebalance, and start one around a frozen component. Ownership moves in two steps and cannot be renounced. |
| Frozen components | If an issuer pauses or blacklists a component, holders take every other component out with `redeemSkipping` and claim the frozen one later. The guardian rebalances around it with a keep mask, which neither moves the component nor reads its price. |

## Not built yet

- `createAndMint` in one call.
- Constituent changes: a new list is a new index.
- Legs that split one swap across V3 and V4, and per-pair partial-fill auctions.
- A backstop for auctions too small to pay. The keeper fills only what pays, unless its operator sets a subsidy.
- An aggregator or Universal Router 2.1 zap, and V4 pools on the Sepolia test market.
- An L2 deployment, if cheap minting for small buyers matters after launch.
